# Vision, principles, and target users

## Refined promise

**NextSet keeps the next useful action obvious—before, during, and after a workout—while preserving the flexibility and record quality serious training requires.**

The promise is intentionally about reducing decision and logging friction, not guaranteeing physical outcomes.

## Product character

NextSet should feel fast, purposeful, clean, athletic, trustworthy, quietly fun, and satisfying under repeated use. It should not feel like a social feed, AI coach, generic dashboard, game, punishment system, or dense analytics product.

## Target users

### New gym users

Need a clear next workout, understandable language, sensible programme defaults, confidence during set entry, and progress feedback that does not assume training expertise. Advanced concepts remain available but translated and progressively disclosed.

### Intermediate users

Need fast set logging, programme continuity, previous-performance context, exercise history, optional progression guidance, and easy adaptation when time or equipment changes.

### Experienced lifters

Need custom exercises and programmes, precise set types, RPE/RIR, editable completed sessions, deterministic progression rules, reliable export, and historical integrity without crowding the default path.

### General fitness users

Need a straightforward, durable training record across free weights, machines, bodyweight, and mixed routines, without being pushed into specialised powerlifting, bodybuilding, or coaching language.

### People with inconsistent schedules

Need flexible sequences, rescheduling, intentional skips, repeats, recovery days, short-workout adaptation, and explicit control over whether skipped work returns later.

## Jobs to be done

- When I open the app to train, tell me what comes next and let me start without rebuilding my plan.
- When I am between sets, show the minimum context needed to record accurately and continue.
- When equipment or time changes, help me preserve the purpose of the workout without taking control away.
- When performance changes, show what happened and why a suggestion is being made.
- When life interrupts training, keep the programme coherent without guilt or silent schedule changes.
- When I rely on the app as my training record, keep my data safe, editable, exportable, and available offline.

## Product principles

1. **Optimise the fiftieth workout.** Repeated set entry, correction, navigation, and interruption recovery carry more weight than novelty.
2. **Simple first, powerful when requested.** Defaults teach by doing; expert controls remain close but quiet.
3. **One useful answer at a time.** Every metric or chart must answer a stated user question.
4. **Assist without controlling.** Users can reschedule, skip, repeat, substitute, shorten, edit, and override.
5. **Explain suggestions.** Show the observed condition, programme rule, and proposed action.
6. **Encourage without manipulation.** Celebrate useful milestones; never use shame, fear, streak loss, or body pressure.
7. **Local reliability is a feature.** The device record is immediately durable; sync is additive, not a prerequisite.
8. **Preserve training meaning.** Adaptations should retain movement, muscle, workload, or programme intent and explain any compromise.
9. **Accessible under movement and fatigue.** Large targets, readable numbers, strong hierarchy, redundant cues, and reduced motion are defaults.
10. **Earn complexity.** A feature enters the product only when it solves a prioritised problem, has a measurable outcome, and fits the phase.

## Product anti-goals

- Copying a competitor’s layout, language, branding, or distinctive assets
- Optimising for screenshots at the expense of repeated use
- Treating adherence as moral performance
- Autonomous programme or load decisions presented as authority
- A public attention economy or engagement-maximising feed
- A cloud dependency for core workout logging
- Mainstream scope distorted around one user, sport, routine, or schedule

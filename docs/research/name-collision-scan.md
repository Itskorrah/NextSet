# “NextSet” name collision scan

**Research date:** 6 August 2026 (Australia/Sydney)  
**Status:** **Red flag — multiple exact-name fitness products already exist.**

> This is a product naming and discoverability scan, **not legal advice, a trademark search or trademark clearance**. It is incomplete by design. A qualified trademark professional must assess registrability, infringement risk, classes, territories and common-law use before any public commitment.

## Executive finding

The working name “NextSet” collides not merely with adjacent software, but with several current workout planners/loggers on the same app stores and web search surface. Two Apple listings and at least two Google Play listings use the exact name for substantially overlapping strength-training jobs. A separate web product also presents itself as a NextSet resistance-training app.

**Product recommendation:** stop treating NextSet as a plausible public name. Keep it only as an internal codename while a naming sprint and professional clearance run in parallel. The direct category collisions alone create unacceptable App Store optimisation, search, word-of-mouth, support, review and mistaken-identity risk even before legal analysis.

That recommendation is a product-risk inference; it is not a conclusion that a particular party owns enforceable rights or that the name is legally unavailable.

## Trademark signals reviewed

A selected public USPTO proceeding result shows an application for **NEXTSET FITNESS** (serial 88351700) associated with an opposition/abandonment record in [TTABVUE](https://ttabvue.uspto.gov/ttabvue/v?pnam=Jason+Miniel++). An older NEXTSET software registration is shown as cancelled by a [third-party record](https://trademarks.justia.com/758/84/nextset-75884262.html).

These records are **signals only**:

- an abandoned application does not grant permission to use the wording;
- a cancelled registration does not prove that no one has enforceable rights;
- the records may cover different owners, goods/services, dates and territories;
- direct current marketplace use can matter independently of a federal registration;
- this scan did not assess similarity of marks, priority, consent, coexistence or likelihood of confusion.

No legal conclusion should be drawn from these snippets.

## Product and go-to-market impact

### App stores

- Exact-name strength apps can occupy branded-search results before launch.
- Ratings/reviews and screenshots can be attributed to the wrong product.
- Support messages, refund complaints and privacy concerns can cross products.
- Store reviewers may require a more distinguishing display name even where a binary identifier is unique.
- A later rename would fragment links, screenshots, word of mouth and early product memory.

### Web and discovery

- The obvious domains and phrase variants are already distributed across unrelated owners.
- Search engines must disambiguate multiple fitness products using the same high-intent words: NextSet, workout, gym, strength, programme and tracker.
- Backlinks, social mentions and AI/search summaries are likely to merge products.
- An exact-name branded query would not be reliably navigational.

### Trust and identity

- A local-first privacy promise is harder to establish if users cannot tell which privacy policy or app listing is official.
- A distinct support channel and data-deletion path become harder to communicate.
- Visual distinction cannot solve spoken referrals such as “download NextSet.”

## Decision

| Option | Assessment | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| Continue publicly as **NextSet** | Direct same-category exact-name collisions on both major app-store ecosystems | **Reject without extraordinary legal and brand evidence** |
| Add a descriptive suffix, e.g. “NextSet Training” | Still collides semantically with existing NextSet fitness uses and may remain weak in search | **Do not assume this solves the problem** |
| Keep **NextSet** as an internal codename only | No public discoverability promise; low short-term switching cost | **Accept temporarily** |
| Create a new distinctive name and clear it | More work now, materially lower downstream confusion/rebrand cost | **Recommended** |

## Naming sprint criteria

A replacement should:

1. Be distinctive when spoken, not only when paired with “app”.
2. Avoid descriptive dependence on `set`, `rep`, `lift`, `fit`, `gym` or `strong`; these can still appear in a descriptor.
3. Work for a private journal and planning product without implying medical coaching or artificial intelligence.
4. Be pronounceable in the initial target markets and robust to common spelling variants.
5. Have a plausible App Store/Google Play display-name path, web domain strategy and social/support identity.
6. Survive a collision screen across fitness, software, health, coaching and adjacent sports.
7. Pass professional trademark clearance in the actual launch jurisdictions and relevant classes.

The naming team should produce 20–30 broad candidates, narrow to 5–8 on product/linguistic criteria, then commission clearance before visual identity or public repository/package names harden.

## Search coverage and limitations

This scan reviewed broad web search, selected Apple App Store and Google Play results, direct domains and selected US trademark-result pages. It did **not** complete:

- a comprehensive USPTO Trademark Search/assignment/common-law investigation;
- WIPO Global Brand Database, EUIPO, UKIPO, IP Australia or other jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction clearance;
- class-by-class goods/services analysis;
- company/business-name registries;
- exhaustive domain variants, historical WHOIS or aftermarket availability;
- social handles, package registries, GitHub organisations and podcast/newsletter names exhaustively;
- phonetic, spelling, translation, cultural or linguistic screening;
- owner/contact, first-use, geographic reach, marketplace reputation or coexistence analysis;
- counsel review of similarity and likelihood of confusion.

Search results are dynamic, localised and personalised. Store products can be unpublished or unavailable in a researcher’s region. A professional search should refresh all results immediately before filing or launch.

## Safe next step

Use “NextSet” only as a private working codename, begin the naming sprint now, and give the shortlist plus this collision evidence to qualified trademark counsel. Do not buy a visual identity, publish store metadata or announce the name while clearance is unresolved.
